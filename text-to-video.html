ClipDuel · Make videos · ← back to the first-run guide

Write a paragraph. Get up to four styled videos of it.

Paste a script, or a one-line brief that AI turns into a script. ClipDuel storyboards it into scenes, draws each scene, narrates it, and assembles a captioned video. Add a photo and your own character stars in every scene. Add an image and the art takes on its style.

STEP 1 OF 6

Write the script, or just the idea

Choose what your text is.

  • The script: narrated word for word. The counter estimates each video's length.
  • A brief: AI writes a 60–120-word narration script from it. That script then drives everything: scenes, captions, voice, and imagery.

The script is written once per job and saved, so every video in the job narrates the same words. That keeps side-by-side comparisons fair.

STEP 2 OF 6

Pick a look for each video

Make 1 to 4 videos from the same text. Each one gets four settings.

  • Style: one of eight presets, or Style reference (step 4).
  • Aspect: portrait, landscape, or square.
  • Voice: a voice on your Mac, or an OpenAI voice ✨.
  • Imagery: procedural (drawn locally, free) or AI imagery ✨.

When your videos differ in exactly one setting, like the highlighted style column here, your pick in step 6 tells you which value of that setting you prefer. If they differ in more than one, it only tells you which whole video you liked.

Swatches suggest each preset's mood; they are not renders. With procedural imagery, a preset draws seeded SVG motifs for free. With AI imagery, the preset's art-direction text steers the image model. Each preset also carries its own caption typography.

STEP 3 OF 6 · optional

Put your character in every scene

Upload a photo of your subject: a pet, a product, a mascot. Every AI-imagery scene re-draws that subject in the video's art style, and it stays consistent from scene to scene.

  • A vision model first describes the subject's appearance, and only its appearance, never its pose or surroundings.
  • Each scene image is generated with the photo attached. The scene writer must repeat that description word for word and is barred from inventing features.
  • Needs at least one video set to AI imagery, or the job is rejected.

The describe-first step fixes a real bug. Without it, the text-only scene writer made up a different cat for each style, and that text outweighed the photo.

STEP 4 OF 6 · optional

Borrow the art style of any image

Upload a second image that shows the look you want. Then set one or more videos' style to Style reference. It sits in the same menu as the eight presets, so you can put Claymation side by side with your own style.

  • A vision model describes only the image's style: medium, palette, texture, line, and lighting. Naming its subjects is explicitly banned.
  • That description replaces the preset's art direction. The image itself isn't sent to the image model.
  • Captions switch to a neutral set (Helvetica, light fill, amber accent), since the art direction is yours.

Works with the character reference. In the live acceptance test, a cat photo plus a synthwave image produced the same cat against a purple dusk, a striped sun, and a cyan grid.

STEP 5 OF 6

Set the voice and the music

  • Narration is on by default at 100%. Turn it off for a captions-only video. Scene timing then comes from word count (2.5 words per second), and the voice pickers are disabled.
  • Background music is optional: MP3, M4A, or WAV up to 15 MB, 40% by default. It loops to fit the video, fades out over the last 2 seconds, and sits under the narration at your exact slider levels.

With narration off, videos that differ only in voice would render identically, so that combination is rejected at submit.

STEP 6 OF 6

Render, then pick a favorite

The job card shows your references, your brief, and the script AI wrote from it. Each video appears as soon as it finishes.

  • Pick my favorite shows your videos two at a time, with names hidden. Three videos take two picks. The winner's look is preselected for your next job.
  • Portrait videos get a Post button for Instagram Reels and TikTok.
  • Re-running the same job costs nothing. Every AI result is cached.

How your text becomes scenes

Scenes are cut from the script without any AI, so the same text always yields the same storyboard. Each sentence becomes a scene. A sentence over 18 words splits at commas, semicolons, or dashes. A fragment under 4 words merges into its neighbor. Each scene's highlighted caption word is its longest word that isn't a stop word. Edit the text to see it re-cut.
